# Anchour Brand Voice Rubric

The scoring logic for the Anchour brand voice consistency checker. Grounded in
Anchour's real website copy (`voice-samples.txt`), not generic brand-voice
advice.

**How to use this rubric:** Score a piece of copy on each of the six
dimensions below from **1 to 5**, where:

- **5** — Textbook Anchour. Reads like it came off the site.
- **3** — Neutral. Not wrong, but not distinctly Anchour either.
- **1** — Actively off-voice. Reads like generic agency marketing.

A passing overall score is **≥ 4 average with no single dimension below 3.** A
piece can be grammatically fine and still fail — "off-voice" is the failure
mode this tool exists to catch.

---

## 1. Sentence Rhythm & Economy

**Definition:** Sentences run short and declarative, using fragments and
em-dashes for cadence and punch rather than long, clause-stacked corporate
sentences.

- **Good** *(from samples):*
  > Flash in the pan, one-hit wonder — we're not your marketing agency. We build for sustained growth.
- **Bad:**
  > As a full-service agency, we offer a comprehensive suite of integrated marketing solutions designed to help businesses of all sizes achieve their strategic objectives across a wide range of channels and touchpoints.

**How to score:** Reward short, varied sentence lengths, fragments, and
em-dashes used for rhythm; penalize run-ons of 25+ words and multi-clause
sentences that bury the point.

---

## 2. Concrete Over Abstract

**Definition:** Meaning is carried by tangible, physical nouns and metaphors,
not abstract business nouns.

- **Good** *(from samples):*
  > We make brands with heft: good bones, strong foundations, nice people.
- **Bad:**
  > We deliver holistic, value-driven brand solutions that optimize engagement and unlock your organization's full potential.

**How to score:** Count physical/sensory nouns and metaphors (heft, bones,
foundations, staying power) against abstract buzzwords (solutions, potential,
value, synergy, engagement-as-noun). Verbs count too — reward specific verbs
(build, make, transform, accelerate) over generic ones (help, provide, deliver,
enable). Concrete language should outweigh abstract by a clear margin.

---

## 3. Conviction & Stance

**Definition:** The copy takes a clear position and stands behind it — even to
the point of saying who it's *not* for — without hedging or qualifiers.

- **Good** *(from samples):*
  > If you can't look away, we're doing it right.
- **Bad:**
  > We strive to be one of the leading agencies that may help support your brand's potential growth objectives.

**How to score:** Reward declarative claims, a willing point of view, and
exclusionary confidence ("we're not your marketing agency"). Penalize hedges
("strive to," "may," "one of the leading," "help support," "aim to") and any
sentence that qualifies itself into meaninglessness.

---

## 4. Substance Over Hype

**Definition:** Value is framed as durability, craft, and staying power — never
as flash, novelty, or empty superlatives.

- **Good** *(from samples):*
  > Building brands with purpose, scale, and staying power.
- **Bad:**
  > We're a cutting-edge, world-class agency delivering game-changing, next-level results that will revolutionize your brand overnight.

**How to score:** Flag empty superlatives (world-class, cutting-edge,
game-changing, best-in-class, revolutionary) and speed/novelty hype
("overnight," "in days"). Reward language about foundations, longevity, and
sustained results. Anchour explicitly rejects the "flash in the pan" — hype is
a hard fail here.

---

## 5. Warmth With a Moral Center

**Definition:** Confidence is paired with a human, good-faith warmth — the copy
cares about doing good and roots for the right kind of client.

- **Good** *(from samples):*
  > We build brands with purpose—design to grow, engage, and do good. We're here to help the good ones win.
- **Bad:**
  > We maximize market dominance and drive aggressive returns for our clients.

**How to score:** Look for warmth and a values signal ("do good," "nice
people," "the good ones," "help ... win") sitting alongside the confidence.
Penalize copy that is purely transactional, cold, or aggressive with no human
dimension — but don't reward soft, gushy, or slangy filler either. Warm and
direct, not warm and fuzzy.

---

## 6. Partner Voice (We + You)

**Definition:** The copy speaks as a collaborator, leading with "we" and pulling
"you" into a shared effort — the agency and the client building something
together.

- **Good** *(from samples):*
  > If you're ready to stand out in your market and redefine success, you're in the right spot. Let's make something great together.
- **Bad:**
  > The company provides its clients with best-in-class deliverables tailored to their business requirements.

**How to score:** Reward first-person plural + second-person address ("we,"
"you," "let's," "together"). Penalize detached third-person framing ("the
company," "clients," "businesses," "customers") and copy that describes the
agency from the outside instead of speaking as it.

---

## Scoring Summary

| # | Dimension | Fails when… |
|---|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Sentence Rhythm & Economy | Long, clause-stacked corporate run-ons |
| 2 | Concrete Over Abstract | Abstract buzzwords and generic verbs dominate |
| 3 | Conviction & Stance | Hedged, qualified, afraid to take a position |
| 4 | Substance Over Hype | Empty superlatives, novelty/speed hype |
| 5 | Warmth With a Moral Center | Cold, transactional, or purely aggressive |
| 6 | Partner Voice (We + You) | Detached third-person; no "we"/"you" |
